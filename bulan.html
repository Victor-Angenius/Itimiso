01 · Story

July 1969, Tranquility Base

Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin landed aboard Eagle, then spent about two and a half hours outside. Before heading home they placed science instruments, a flag, and a memorial plaque.

Between 1969 and 1972, six Apollo missions landed on the Moon. In total, about 382 kg of rock and soil were brought home. To make room for those samples, the astronauts left behind equipment they no longer needed.

Every item left behind was a trade: one kilogram of equipment meant one kilogram of rock that could come home.

03 · Science that endures

The mirrors that still work

Retroreflectors need no electricity. They simply reflect light back toward its source. For as long as the Moon stays up there, observers on Earth can fire a laser at it and measure how long the light takes to go and return.

1

Fire the laser

Observatories on Earth shoot a powerful laser pulse toward the reflector site, about 384,400 km away.

2

Bounce straight back

The corner-cube mirrors return the light exactly toward its origin, no matter the angle it arrived from.

3

Time the round trip

The round trip takes about 2.5 seconds. From that timing, the Earth-Moon distance is known to within millimetres.
